## Post 2 — what stays scarce (Sat Sep 6)

Software used to be the expensive part.

[A concrete moment from your building years: what a thing cost or how
long it took, then vs. now.]

That world is gone. The distance between an idea and working software
has collapsed. Which raises the question every builder should be asking:
if software is cheap, what stays scarce?

For a local practice, I keep arriving at the same three answers.

The owner's voice. Patients can smell generic content a mile away.
The owner's evenings. No tool matters if it adds work instead of
removing it.
Trust. In healthcare, one careless claim can cost more than a year of
marketing earns.

Notice that none of these are software problems. They are judgment
problems. Which means the opportunity was never "another AI writing
tool." It was a system that carries the judgment: voice, compliance, and
approval built in, so the owner stays in control without staying up
late.

That is the bet I made. Next post, I'll show you the evidence.

## Post 4 — the human gate (Thu Sep 11)

The most important feature I built is a button that stops everything.

Nothing my engine produces reaches the public until the owner approves
it. Not an article, not a caption, not an email. In most software, a
human approval step is friction to eliminate. In healthcare marketing,
it is the entire point.

Health advertising has rules for good reasons. No outcome promises. No
invented patient stories. No claims your license can't stand behind. An
AI that publishes unsupervised in this space is not a productivity win.
It's a liability with a subscription fee.

So the system does the eighty percent that eats your evenings: research,
writing, design, scheduling, distribution. The owner does the twenty
percent only they can do: judgment. Five minutes with a cup of coffee,
approve or reject, done.

[Optional: a real moment where the review gate caught something, stated
generally.]

Machines that show up daily. Humans that stay accountable. That's the
shape I believe local practice marketing takes from here.
