# The Practice Autopilot Walkthrough — Video Script + Storyboard

**Status: DRAFT v1 for review — 2026-08-06**

A complete pitch AND a system walkthrough in one video: the viewer arrives from
the X-Ray funnel (or the sales letter) already holding their leak number; this
video is the evidence, then the ask. Voice = the master pitch. One CTA: the
buy button under the video. No chapters to skip to... it plays start to finish.

## Length philosophy

Say what needs saying; hard cap **under 20 minutes** (attention ceiling).
This draft: ~2,525 VO words ≈ **17:25 of narration**, landing **~18:00–19:00** (trim pass available if audio runs long)
with demo breathing room. The runtime is locked by the AUDIO recording —
screen capture is edited to fit the audio afterwards.

**After audio is produced:** run the "minute-claim sweep" — every asset
currently says "12-minute walkthrough" and gets the real number:
master pitch §7 · both sales pages (hero/close/header refs) · /walkthrough
hero + metadata · X-Ray results CTA + "under" line · Debrief PDF template +
static print HTML (CTA box) + TEMPLATE_VERSION bump · nurture emails 5/6/9/10
+ SMS 3 · outreach doc. Then one email rebuild + single GHL import.

## SCENE 2 · Why this is happening · ~1:15 · VO ~185 words

**ON SCREEN:** Title card "Three forces." Then: a phone lock screen at 9:04pm →
Google Maps pack for "chiropractor near me" → the stat band (78% / 100× /
2 days) as on the site.

**VO:**
First, understand that the leak is not your fault. Three forces created it,
and none of them asked your permission.

The technology force. Every patient in your town now carries every
competitor's front desk in their pocket. When someone's back seizes at 9pm,
they don't wait for your opening hours... they search, they message, and they
book with whoever answers. Google decides who exists. The map pack decides
who gets the call.

The social force. Patients stopped calling back. Not because they're rude...
because Amazon, Uber and a decade of instant everything trained them.
Seventy-eight percent of customers buy from whoever answers first. Reach an
inquiry inside five minutes and you're a hundred times more likely to even
get them on the line than half an hour later. The average business takes two
days.

And the economic force. The compounding asset in healthcare used to be
location and reputation. Now it's attention and reviews. The practices
growing right now are not better clinicians than you... they run better
systems.

## SCENE 3 · The belief shift · ~1:00 · VO ~150 words

**ON SCREEN:** Title card "It's not a discipline problem." A calendar
grid with content tasks slipping week by week (simple animation), then the
line "Attention runs 24 hours a day. Humans don't." full-frame.

**VO:**
Now, you've already tried the obvious fix. It's called trying harder.

Posting at 11pm... telling the front desk to follow up when it's quiet...
asking for a Google review when you remember, from the patients you're sure
will say yes. And the expensive practice coaches will happily sell you a
system for getting consistent.

But then you hit the actual problem. It's not a discipline problem. It's a
physics problem. Attention now runs twenty-four hours a day. Humans don't.
You need sleep. Your family needs you. You cannot out-hustle a force that
never sleeps... you can only out-system it.

The practices that grow are simply the ones where the system runs anyway...
whether anyone had a good week or not.

## SCENE 4 · Patient drift · ~1:00 · VO ~150 words

**ON SCREEN:** An appointment book thinning over months (subtle animation).
Then a phone contact list scrolling... no chiropractor saved. Google Maps
again.

**VO:**
And the leak this physics creates is the quietest one in your business...
patient drift.

A patient finishes their care plan. They feel good... which means your care
worked. Then nothing happens. No contact, no content, no reason to think of
you. Eight months later their back flares up, in a city full of clinics that
answer faster than you.

Quick question. How many electricians do you have saved in your phone for
emergencies? And when the lights go out and nobody answers... you call the
first one on Google Maps that responds. Correct? That is exactly how your
faded patients choose their next chiropractor.

Patients don't leave. They fade. And fading is invisible in the appointment
book until the quarter is already soft.
